# Motion: abm

## Overview
A coordinated, multi-channel campaign against a curated list of named accounts. ABM is account-based,
not lead-based: the unit of work is the account and its buying committee, not a single contact. It is
the highest-touch, highest-effort motion, reserved for a small set of strategic accounts where the
deal size justifies the coordination.

## Trigger
A named account list, curated separately from the ICP output. Accounts are not auto-enrolled from
audience-builder; sales and marketing agree the target list, and membership is deliberate.

## Anchor style
Multi-channel coordinated: email plus LinkedIn, with optional direct mail. The message is consistent
across the buying committee (economic buyer, champion, influencer), so the account experiences one
coherent campaign rather than disconnected touches.

## Cadence
Quarterly campaign: 5 to 7 touches over 6 weeks, spread across channels.
- Weeks 1 to 6: touches paced roughly weekly, alternating email and LinkedIn, direct mail optional at
  a high-value moment.
Break-up rule: hold at the end of the 6-week campaign, review account engagement, and re-enroll next
quarter if the account is still a target.

## Tier defaults
Not tier-driven. ABM is list-driven, so membership comes from the curated named-account list rather
than from a tier. In practice these are strategic Tier A accounts, but the tier does not enroll them;
the list does.

## Approval requirements
Human-approve every touch. These are high-value, high-visibility accounts, and every channel touch is
reviewed before it goes out.

## Suppression rules
Strict, plus an exclusivity rule: an ABM account is never in another concurrent sequence while the
campaign is active. The coordinated campaign owns the account for its duration.

## Success signals
Measured at the account level, not the contact level: buying-committee engagement (multiple personas
engaging), meeting rate with the committee, pipeline created, and opportunity conversion. Single-contact
reply rate is a weak proxy here.
